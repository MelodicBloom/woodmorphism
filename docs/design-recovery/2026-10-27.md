## Repository-specific records

### D10 — Woodmorphism: grain, growth rings, and visible joints

**Use when:** When designing Woodmorphism components, organic interfaces, structural hierarchy, or a material-family adaptation.

[Recovered intent] Woodmorphism's governing questions are structural: grain gives a dominant reading axis; growth rings express increasing depth/specificity; visible joints reveal component boundaries; warmth originates in the palette and hierarchy. Its README explicitly avoids borrowing the glass/metal aesthetic of sibling systems.

[Design DNA] Preserve birch/maple highlights, oak surfaces, walnut cores, ebony shadows, ring-depth tones, restrained warm shadows, visible seams, and organic settling. Its named token examples include --wood-light, --wood-mid, --wood-deep, --wood-grain, --ring-*, --shadow-joint, and --t-settle.

[Observed boundary] The inspected tree has two files. The detailed README is stronger evidence of intended rules than of a mature component library. Do not count its proposed component directory as implemented.

[Repair resolution — proposed] Define concrete reading, nesting, focus, press, and density rules. Label mappings such as grain → hierarchy as design interpretations rather than physical laws. Use real grain only when it improves material identity without degrading text.

[Reimagined horizon] An instrument-like reading/workspace system for archives, maker tools, or grounded editorial products, with species/density variants and visible assembly rather than a collection of brown cards.

[Carry-forward test] A nested reading view makes parent/child structure legible without relying on texture or color alone. One native input and button retain visible focus, disabled, error, and mobile behavior.

## Practical continuation contract

1. Re-read current repository instructions and source before implementation. The baseline above identifies this publication, not a permanent execution HEAD.
2. Retain the full design horizon separately from the current demonstrator. Name what the demonstrator preserves, approximates, defers, or cannot prove.
3. Map tokens and controls to actual consumers with explicit units, ownership, accessible alternatives, and observable effect.
4. Complete the relevant interaction/error/cancellation states before promoting a reusable component.
5. Pair source/runtime checks with human correspondence review against the actual visual inspiration.
6. Keep proposed repairs unexecuted until a later implementation records its own revision, checks, and evidence.
